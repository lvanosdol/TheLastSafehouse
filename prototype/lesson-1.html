Meet and rebuild the computer

Simulated workbench — nothing here touches a real computer. Meet the eight parts, match each one to its purpose, then rebuild the computer on its own page.

Mara “Before we put this computer back together, let’s meet its parts. You don’t need to know everything yet—we’ll take them one at a time.”

Before you open a real computer:

  • Shut the computer down and unplug it before opening anything.
  • Never open a power supply. It holds a dangerous charge even after unplugging.
  • Discharge static by touching a grounded metal surface, or wear a strap, before handling parts.
  • Never force a component or connector. If it does not fit easily, stop and check the manual.
  • Leave work you are not confident in to a qualified professional.

This simulation teaches the concepts. It is not a guide to dismantling your own computer.

How this works

  1. Meet the parts on the bench.
  2. Match each part to what it does.
  3. Rebuild the computer and boot it.

Meet the parts on the bench

Eight parts, one at a time. Open each to read what it is for, where it connects, and one thing worth knowing. Nothing to memorise: the matching comes next, and these entries stay here while you do it.

Match each part to what it does

Now the same eight parts, with their purposes shuffled. Pick the purpose for every one. The entries above are still there if you need them, and a wrong answer costs nothing — the lesson says what the mix-up was, and you try again as often as you like.

Drag a part onto the purpose it belongs to. From the keyboard: press Enter on a part to pick it up, Tab to a purpose, press Enter again to place it.
