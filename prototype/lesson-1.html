Meet and rebuild the computer

Mara “Before we put this computer back together, let’s meet its parts. You don’t need to know everything yet — we’ll take them one at a time.”

How this works

  1. Match each part to what it does.
  2. Read the safety card before touching anything.
  3. Rebuild the computer and boot it.

The parts are laid out on the bench in front of you. Each one below can be opened to read what it is for; you can come back to them at any point in the lesson.

The parts on the bench

Eight parts, one at a time. Open one to read its purpose, where it connects, and one thing worth knowing.

Motherboard

Connects the components and lets them communicate.

Where it connects
Onto the standoffs inside the case, with everything else attached to it.
Worth knowing
Every other part on this bench ends up plugged into this board.
Processor

Carries out the instructions the computer is given.

Where it connects
Into the socket on the motherboard, under the cooling assembly.
Worth knowing
It has one correct orientation, marked by a small triangle in the corner.
RAM

Temporary working memory for the programs that are running.

Where it connects
The memory slots on the motherboard, pushed in until the clips close.
Worth knowing
Its contents are gone when the power is off. Files are kept by storage, not by RAM.
Storage

Keeps files after the power is off.

Where it connects
An SSD fits an M.2 slot on the board; a hard drive connects by cable.
Worth knowing
Windows itself lives here, which is why the computer needs it to start.
Power supply

Provides power to the whole system.

Where it connects
Feeds the board through the 24-pin cable and the processor through its own cable.
Worth knowing
It is never opened, even unplugged: it holds a dangerous charge. That is on the safety card.
Graphics card

Produces the picture for the monitor.

Where it connects
The long PCIe slot, with the monitor plugged into the card rather than the board.
Worth knowing
A monitor in the motherboard’s video port stays blank when a card is installed.
Cooling

Stops the components overheating.

Where it connects
Onto the processor, with its fan cable to the labelled header on the board.
Worth knowing
Fans starting is one of the first signs of life when the power goes on.
Case

Holds and protects the parts.

Where it connects
Everything mounts inside it. The Safehouse chassis arrives with its buttons and lights already wired.
Worth knowing
Its power button only does anything once the power supply is switched on.

Peripherals — the monitor, keyboard and mouse — come later, when you plug them in.
