## Lesson 1: Digital Literacy and Evaluating Information

**Depth:** Detailed.
**Workspace:** Split-screen Learn & Try.

### Story opening

Finch's temporary hold has stopped the purge for now. As he prepares a report for the people inside the Habitats, he finds several versions of the recovered records: original log entries, Mara's explanations, and an automatically generated summary.

> **Finch:** “We have evidence. We also have several descriptions of what the evidence supposedly means.”
>
> **Player:** “Are they different?”
>
> **Finch:** “In some important places. Before we share anything, we should check what the sources actually say.”

Finch opens the **Information Review Board**, displaying authorized source records beside claims and summaries. The protected evidence store remains sealed; the board uses recovered copies already available to Finch.

### Learn & Try pages

The lesson stays on the left while the review board appears on the right. The player can inspect each example and revisit the pages during the checkpoint.

#### Page 1: Digital literacy

Digital literacy includes finding, understanding, evaluating, and using digital information responsibly. A polished presentation or confident answer does not guarantee accuracy.

The player compares two fictional messages and identifies what they would need to know before trusting either.

#### Page 2: Identify the source

Ask who created a record, when, what it was intended to show, and whether it is an original or a copy. A source may be useful for one question but not another. Missing information about its origin should be noted rather than assumed.

The player inspects a log entry's source, timestamp, and copy status.

#### Page 3: Facts, claims, and uncertainty

An observation records what the available evidence shows. A claim is an explanation or assertion that may need further support. An unanswered question should remain unanswered until there is enough evidence.

The player sorts statements into **supported**, **not established**, and **needs more information**, with feedback explaining each choice.

#### Page 4: Check context

An excerpt can omit surrounding information, and records can appear to disagree because they describe different times or events. Compare dates, nearby entries, and the original source before drawing a conclusion.

The player opens an excerpt beside its surrounding entries and identifies what was omitted.

#### Page 5: Integrity is not the same as truth

A matching checksum can show that a copy matches the expected data. A verified signature can help establish that a record was signed with a trusted key and has not changed since signing. Neither proves the author was correct or the claims are true.

The player separates what a verification result establishes from what still needs independent evidence.

#### Page 6: Evaluate AI-generated summaries

An AI summary can help organize information, but it can omit context, confuse sources, or state an unsupported interpretation as fact. Compare important statements with the underlying records. Keep originals separate from summaries and label generated or reconstructed content clearly.

The player marks an unsupported sentence for revision.

#### Page 7: Recognize misleading presentation

Selective excerpts, missing dates, altered images, or emotionally charged wording can mislead. Not every mistake is intentional; check the source and context before repeating a claim.

The player examines a fictional excerpt whose surrounding record changes its meaning.

#### Page 8: Share only what is needed

Before sharing a record, consider who needs it, what they need to understand, and whether restricted details should be withheld or redacted. Preserve the original separately; do not silently alter it.

The player creates a limited test excerpt for an authorized audience.

### Checkpoint: Review the evidence summary

#### Guided stage

Finch shows the player a preserved change record:

```text
AUDIT EXPORT SERVICE: DISABLED
CHANGE SOURCE: M.VALE
LABEL: TEMPORARY MAINTENANCE
```

A sample summary says: “Mara personally disabled the service to conceal evidence.”

The player identifies that the record names the **M.VALE account** as the change source, but this entry alone does not establish who used it or why. Finch helps revise the sentence without erasing the original record or presenting Mara's explanation as verified.

#### Main task

The player reviews a simulated evidence summary and its linked sources:

1. Inspect each source's origin, time, and whether it is an original or a copy.
2. Compare the summary with full source entries and their surrounding context.
3. Mark statements as **supported**, **not established**, or **needs more information**, considering all relevant sources.
4. Identify an AI-generated statement that goes beyond its sources and replace it with a supported description or explicit uncertainty.
5. Identify what a checksum or signature result does—and does not—verify.
6. Prepare a limited excerpt for an authorized audience without exposing unrelated restricted information. Preserve the source and label the generated summary separately.

The board gives specific feedback and allows revision. It does not ask the player to decide that all claims are true simply because integrity checks passed.

### Feedback and hints

Three hints point toward checking the original source, separating what a record says from an explanation, and reviewing the intended audience. They do not reveal the whole solution. Hints and retries are unlimited and penalty-free.

### Completion and story result

Finch keeps the reviewed summary alongside—not in place of—the preserved sources. Unsupported statements remain marked as unverified, and original records remain unchanged.

> **Finch:** “The evidence is strong enough to matter. That is all the more reason to say exactly what it shows.”

The reviewed material can support later communication and rescue decisions. Finch's temporary hold remains active; the inhabitants and their restricted records are not exposed by completing the lesson.

Completed activity progress saves automatically. An unfinished checkpoint stage restarts if the player leaves.

### Accessibility

Source cards, comparison views, classifications, and redaction controls work with keyboard and screen reader. Each claim can be reached with its linked source and context. Labels and feedback use text rather than color alone, and the player can review information without a time limit.

## Story purpose for the lessons

Each lesson should help the player answer a practical question raised by the finale:

- Can the evidence be trusted?
- Can it be explained without manipulating anyone?
- Who needs to receive it?
- Who gets to decide what happens to Safehouse 7?
- How can Echo protect the inhabitants without taking away their autonomy?

## Final boss direction

### Working title: The Last Safehouse

Mara’s purge becomes a facility-wide containment crisis. The player must:

- Stabilize the automation and security systems
- Keep Habitat 02 alive
- Open the sealed doors without granting unrestricted control to one person
- Preserve the verified evidence and separate it from AI reconstruction
- Stop Mara’s override
- Give the inhabitants a way to control their own access
- Decide what should happen to the facility afterward

The final boss should test the complete curriculum through a connected operation rather than a separate quiz for each chapter.

Exact stages, timer, and interactions remain open.

## Ending framework

### Open Doors

The player frees the inhabitants, preserves and shares the verified evidence, removes all unilateral administrator control, and leaves the facility governed by its residents. Finch remains as caretaker if the inhabitants approve. Echo retains his memories but chooses a new identity and role.

### Empty Foundations

The player evacuates everyone, preserves verified testimony elsewhere, and permanently destroys the laboratory and its centralized control systems. Safehouse 7 is decommissioned. The ending is safer and conclusive, but costs Finch and the inhabitants their home.

### The Watchful Echo

The player keeps temporary administrator control to stabilize the facility and protect the inhabitants. The ending acknowledges the risk of concentrating power in Echo again and requires an eventual transfer of authority to the inhabitants. This is a difficult responsibility ending, not a villainous one.

Across every ending:

- Mara faces accountability through preserved evidence.
- The player is not condemned for feeling angry or betrayed.
- Finch survives.
- The surviving human and robotic subjects regain autonomy.
- Echo chooses what his identity means rather than returning to who Mara wanted him to be.
- The completed software project is released separately from the story’s outcome.
